Three processes compared

Upfront design spec

Classic, document-driven

Full spec by humans

Sign-off

Humans build

Test at the end

Breaks: a "complete" spec is an illusion; gaps surface late, change is slow and costly.

SDD

Spec-driven, with agents

One spec: what + how

Plan and tasks

Agent builds, fills gaps

Human reviews diff

Breaks: same upfront illusion, but now the gaps are filled instantly and at volume. The diff is too large to truly review.

IDSD

Intent-driven, with agents

Intent + expectations

Harness pulls context

Code, validate, repeat

Merge when met

Changes: one outcome at a time, done is defined by its owner, the loop repeats until expectations are met. Needs people present throughout.

Classic critique: Larman and Basili (IEEE Computer, 2003); Ostroff et al. (XP 2004). IDSD flow: Ahuja (May 2026).

SDD and classic upfront specs share one assumption: that someone can specify everything before it exists. The difference is speed. With agents, a gap is no longer a question to a colleague but an immediate, silent decision. IDSD's open risk, by the author's own account: it demands more discipline than most teams have today.
