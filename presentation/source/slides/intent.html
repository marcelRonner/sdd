The argument for intent: split the spec, assign owners

Human owns

Intent

The outcome wanted, with no technology in it. Five parts: description, constraints, failure scenarios, success scenarios, links to other intents.

Human owns

Expectations

The boundary: when the result counts as done, when it has failed, and the limits it must stay inside, in the user's terms.

Harness owns

Context

The how: tech stack, existing system, codebase constraints. Pulled in progressively by the tooling, not dumped into one document.

The agent builds and validates in a loop, but never decides what "done" means. People stay present in the loop instead of approving a large diff at the end.

Source: Ahuja's ICE frame (Intent, Context, Expectations), the basis of intent-driven software development (IDSD).

Practical first step from the article: take one real outcome this week, write the five parts of its intent, and ask an outsider where an agent would still have to guess. The author admits teams are not yet ready for this and that he has not fully solved staying in the loop.
